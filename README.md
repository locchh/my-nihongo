# my-nihongo

**Learn kana by shape and sound — never by romaji.**

## Why

The trap for a beginner is romaji. Read `konnichiwa` for long enough and you
never learn to read こんにちは: the Latin letters become the thing you actually
understand, and the kana stay decoration you skip past. Romaji also quietly
hides what it cannot spell — morae, long vowels, は as *wa* rather than *ha* —
so what you memorize is an approximation filtered through a foreign alphabet.

Three rules follow from that, and they shape every screen here:

1. **The prompt is always Japanese.** Romaji is the answer key, never the thing
   you read from.
2. **The answer stays hidden until you commit.** You have to attempt the sound
   before you can check it. A card you can read passively teaches nothing —
   the retrieval attempt *is* the learning.
3. **Both directions count.** か → *ka* is recognition, and it is reading.
   *ka* → か is production, and it is writing and typing. Recognition can coast
   on vague visual memory; production cannot, which makes it the honest test.

The target is a reflex that runs both ways with no romaji in the middle of it:
shape to sound when you read, sound to shape when you write. Further on the
same thing happens a level up — こんにちは should arrive as a greeting, not as
*konnichiwa* and then a greeting.

Background on the writing system — why there are four scripts, where kana came
from, and what romaji loses — is in [docs/OVERVIEW.md](docs/OVERVIEW.md).
